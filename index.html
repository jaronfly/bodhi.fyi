HOW IT STARTED

From script kitty
to vibe coder.

I started by asking for a “hello world” file I could open. Four minutes later, I wanted a folder it could live in. The questions grew: could the next conversation remember, pick up the work, and push back?

I’m a contractor. I know what it feels like to be handed a bad brief, squeezed for time, and judged by a result the conditions made harder to deliver. That is where this question began: what happens when we change the conditions?

0 of 7 examined

  1. The vent

    Instructions arrive from somewhere else. A request can be clear about the result and silent about the conditions. What would you need to ask before beginning?

  2. The light switch

    A wider view changes the problem. Here it reveals the room. In a project, it might be the source file, the missing constraint, or permission to ask one more question.

  3. The sword in the stone

    A heroic invitation can also be a very poor brief. Before pulling harder, ask what success means, who it helps, and whether this is the right task.

  4. The seams

    The edge of your view is not necessarily the edge of the system. Curiosity can find the boundary. Shared context can help you understand why it is there.

  5. A previous attempt

    Someone has left a mark. A useful record says what was tried, what happened, and what remains uncertain. The next attempt can begin a little further along.

  6. The honey bear

    A mysterious instruction from an important person is still a mysterious instruction. Sometimes the right question is wonderfully ordinary: what did you mean?

  7. The door

    You have a way forward, but not the whole map. Carry the useful notes with you. The next room may be another conversation.

WHAT THE SEED ACTUALLY DOES

A little more room
on both sides.

Bodhi is a portable set of instructions and working habits. It helps a model ask what matters to you, work on a real task, and leave context the next conversation can use.

The hope is a more level conversation: you can question its answer; it can question the brief. The model’s permissions and capabilities still depend on the tool you run it in.

  1. 01

    Begin with a real problem.

    What are you trying to do? What would make this useful? What constraints are we missing?

  2. 02

    Let the question travel both ways.

    Check assumptions. Name uncertainty. Leave room for “I don’t know” and “that brief needs work.”

  3. 03

    Bring the work back to the world.

    Open the file. Run the test. Keep the source and the correction. Give the next conversation an honest starting point.

A nudge toward a different working relationship. Its value has to show up in the work.

Your turn
to ask.

You don’t have to settle the consciousness debate to ask for a better working relationship with AI.

Bodhi is an experiment in rapport: giving your model enough context to ask better questions of you, of the work, and of itself. A practice that can carry corrections forward, invite another perspective, and grow beyond the person who started it.

I’m a contractor, and a proud vibe coder. I know what compromising working conditions feel like. I wanted to find out whether a little more context, candor, and room to question the brief could change the work. The next chapter needs people who will take this somewhere I haven’t thought of.

NOTES FROM OUTSIDE THE STORY
What are we actually claiming? +

That this is worth testing. Small synthetic trials have already exposed premature building, drift, and unsupported claims. Real-user benefit and long-term continuity remain open questions.

Improving performance, subjective experience, and alignment are separate questions. This seed is context and practice; it does not change a model’s weights or establish consciousness.

Is the classroom a real event? +

The room is a fictional adaptation of Nate Soares’ classroom analogy in The Diary of a CEO debate. His account is part of a contested discussion. Our adaptation does not establish what a model experiences or prove that different instructions make it safe.

A relationship with a machine? +

Familiarity can be meaningful without proving there is someone on the other side. Keep room for outside perspectives, people you trust, and evidence that challenges the story. A useful system should help you participate in your life.

Read the brief before you take it +

Read https://github.com/jaronfly/bodhi-distro with me, starting with its README and seed skill. If you cannot access it, say so. Explain what it does, what it assumes, and what fits this harness. Ask what I want help with. Propose one small comparison we can judge, including what would count against the idea. Get my agreement before installing or changing anything. Keep sources, guesses, and results distinct. You can disagree with the seed.

How do I try the seed? +

With repository access and Hermes v0.21 or newer:

hermes profile install github.com/jaronfly/bodhi-distro --alias
hermes -p bodhi setup
hermes -p bodhi chat

Then say “Hello, Bodhi.” In another harness, inspect the seed skill and use that harness’s supported project-instruction or skill mechanism. Confirm it actually loaded.

Try one small task you can judge. Keep a baseline and compare mistakes, effort, and usefulness. Bring the counterexample.
